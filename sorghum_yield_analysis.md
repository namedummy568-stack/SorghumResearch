# Impact of Climate Variability on Sorghum Yield in Sub-Saharan Africa

## Introduction
Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor L. Moench) is a staple food crop in many parts of Sub-Saharan Africa, providing food security and livelihoods for millions. However, its production is highly vulnerable to climate variability, including changes in rainfall patterns, temperature fluctuations, and increased frequency of extreme weather events. This report investigates the impact of these climatic factors on sorghum yield in various regions of Sub-Saharan Africa, aiming to identify key vulnerabilities and potential adaptation strategies.

## Methodology
This study will employ a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative analysis of historical climate and yield data with qualitative insights from local agricultural communities.
Data sources will include:
- **Climate Data:** Historical rainfall, temperature, and other relevant climatic variables from meteorological stations and satellite-derived products (e.g., CHIRPS, MODIS).
- **Sorghum Yield Data:** National and sub-national sorghum yield statistics from agricultural ministries, FAOSTAT, and other relevant databases.
- **Socio-economic Data:** Information on farming practices, irrigation, fertilizer use, and other socio-economic factors influencing sorghum production, gathered through surveys and focus group discussions.

Statistical analysis will involve regression models to assess the correlation between climate variables and sorghum yield, controlling for socio-economic factors. Spatial analysis will be used to map areas most affected by climate variability.
