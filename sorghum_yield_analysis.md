# Impact of Climate Variability on Sorghum Yield in Sub-Saharan Africa

## Introduction
Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor L. Moench) is a staple food crop in many parts of Sub-Saharan Africa, providing food security and livelihoods for millions. However, its production is highly vulnerable to climate variability, including changes in rainfall patterns, temperature fluctuations, and increased frequency of extreme weather events. This report investigates the impact of these climatic factors on sorghum yield in various regions of Sub-Saharan Africa, aiming to identify key vulnerabilities and potential adaptation strategies.

## Methodology
This study will employ a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative analysis of historical climate and yield data with qualitative insights from local agricultural communities.

### Data Sources
- **Climate Data:** Historical rainfall, temperature, and other relevant climatic variables will be sourced from a combination of ground-based meteorological stations (e.g., national meteorological agencies) and high-resolution satellite-derived products (e.g., CHIRPS for precipitation, MODIS for land surface temperature). Data will be collected for the period 1980-2020 to capture long-term trends and inter-annual variability.
- **Sorghum Yield Data:** National and sub-national sorghum yield statistics will be obtained from agricultural ministries (e.g., Ministries of Agriculture in Ethiopia, Kenya, and Sudan), FAOSTAT, and other reputable agricultural databases. Yield data will be disaggregated by administrative regions where available.
- **Socio-economic Data:** Information on farming practices, irrigation, fertilizer use, crop varieties, and other socio-economic factors influencing sorghum production will be gathered through structured household surveys and focus group discussions with smallholder farmers in selected study sites. This qualitative data will provide crucial context for interpreting quantitative findings.

### Statistical Analysis
Quantitative analysis will involve the following steps:
1.  **Data Preprocessing:** Cleaning, standardization, and interpolation of missing data points for both climate and yield datasets.
2.  **Exploratory Data Analysis:** Visualization of trends, anomalies, and spatial patterns in climate variables and sorghum yield.
3.  **Regression Modeling:** Multiple linear regression and panel data models will be employed to assess the relationship between climate variables (e.g., growing season rainfall, temperature extremes) and sorghum yield. Control variables will include socio-economic factors (e.g., fertilizer application rates, access to irrigation, improved seed adoption) to isolate the impact of climate variability.
4.  **Spatial Analysis:** Geographic Information Systems (GIS) will be used to map and analyze the spatial distribution of climate impacts on sorghum yield, identifying hotspots of vulnerability. Techniques such as geographically weighted regression (GWR) may be applied to account for spatial heterogeneity.
5.  **Trend Analysis:** Time-series analysis will be conducted to identify long-term trends in sorghum yield and climate variables, and to assess the frequency and intensity of extreme climate events.
